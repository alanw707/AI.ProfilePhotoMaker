# GPT Image 2.0 Pack Strategy Plan

> Status: Product/implementation plan for pivoting AI Profile Photo Maker from an API-wrapper experience into a verticalized photo outcome engine.
>
> Goal: create a credible path to **$5,000/month** by selling finished photo assets, niche workflows, and business-ready packs instead of generic image generation credits.

## Executive Summary

The product should not compete with OpenAI as a cheaper or simpler prompt box. That is a weak position because users can go directly to OpenAI for generic generation.

The winning position is:

> **Upload casual photos. Get finished, use-case-ready professional image packs that look like you — no prompting, no trial-and-error, no curation work.**

GPT Image 2.0 becomes the quality engine behind the scenes. The product value is the workflow around it: specialized recipes, identity consistency, upload guidance, automatic curation, crop presets, niche-specific packaging, and optional concierge delivery.

## Revenue Target Math

Target: **$5,000/month**.

Potential paths:

- 103 customers/month at $49
- 64 customers/month at $79
- 26 customers/month at $199
- 10 customers/month at $499
- 4 customers/month at $1,250

Recommendation: use a blended model:

- Self-serve consumer packs at $29–$99
- Premium concierge packs at $149–$299
- Team/company bundles at $499–$1,500+
- Affiliate/partner distribution for high-volume niches

This avoids needing 100+ low-ticket customers immediately and lets the product learn from higher-intent buyers.

## Positioning

### Bad Positioning

- “AI image generator”
- “GPT Image wrapper”
- “Buy credits and generate photos”
- “Prompt-based image creation”

### Strong Positioning

- “Professional photo packs for your career, business, and personal brand.”
- “No prompts. No awkward AI results. Just polished images ready for LinkedIn, real estate, dating, teams, and press.”
- “Turn casual uploads into finished, platform-ready image assets.”

### Core Differentiators vs OpenAI Direct

1. **No blank prompt box** — user chooses an outcome.
2. **Photo recipes** — prebuilt prompts, negative prompts, crops, backgrounds, outfit logic, and style constraints.
3. **Identity consistency** — results should look like the same person across many images.
4. **Guided upload quality** — the app tells users which images are usable and why.
5. **Automatic curation** — recommend the best outputs and label them by use case.
6. **Platform-ready exports** — LinkedIn crop, square avatar, vertical story, website hero, transparent cutout.
7. **Niche-specific packs** — buyers understand outcomes faster than credits.
8. **Concierge option** — human-assisted selection and regeneration for premium pricing.

## Product Architecture: From Credits to Packs

The product should still use credits internally if useful, but the customer-facing unit should be a **Pack**.

A Pack is a structured product bundle containing:

- Intended user/niche
- Required input guidance
- Generation recipe set
- Output count
- Background/style variants
- Cropping/export presets
- Best-pick recommendation
- Optional add-ons
- Clear price and turnaround expectation

## Phase 2: Pack Data Model

Introduce a Pack entity/config that can drive UI and generation behavior.

Suggested fields:

```json
{
  "id": "linkedin-executive",
  "name": "LinkedIn / Executive Pack",
  "targetCustomer": "Professionals and founders",
  "priceCents": 7900,
  "outputCount": 12,
  "requiredInputPhotos": 6,
  "styleRecipes": ["studio-gray", "modern-office", "warm-interior"],
  "outfitRecipes": ["business-formal", "business-casual"],

  "bestPickLabels": ["Best LinkedIn", "Best Executive", "Best Approachable"],
  "supportsConcierge": true

```

### Implementation Notes

- Start config-driven before adding complex admin tooling.
- Pack config can live in API or UI initially, but should eventually be API-owned.
- Keep pricing and deliverables consistent between marketing page, checkout, and generation pipeline.

## Phase 3: Guided Upload Quality

The app should help users provide good references because output quality depends heavily on input quality.

### Requirements

- Minimum photo count per pack.
- Upload checklist:
  - face visible
  - good lighting
  - multiple angles
  - no sunglasses
  - no heavy filters
  - avoid group photos
  - avoid blurry/low-resolution images
- Reject or warn on weak uploads.
- Give plain-language feedback:
  - “This photo is too dark.”
  - “Your face is too small in this image.”
  - “Add at least one front-facing photo.”

### MVP Approach

- Start with simple client-side rules and manual copy.
- Add automated quality scoring later.

## Phase 4: Recipe-Based Generation

Replace generic style selection with pack-specific recipe generation.

### Recipe Components

Each recipe should define:

- use case
- scene/background
- outfit direction
- framing/camera crop
- expression/tone
- realism constraints
- identity preservation instructions
- negative prompt constraints
- export preset

### Example Recipe: Realtor Luxury Interior

```md
Use case: Realtor profile photo
Scene: bright luxury home interior, soft natural light
Outfit: polished business casual or blazer
Expression: warm, confident, approachable
Framing: upper-body portrait, eye-level camera
Constraints: realistic skin texture, preserve identity, no plastic smoothing, no distorted hands, no text/logos
Export: square profile crop + vertical social crop
```

## Phase 5: Curation + Labels

The product should not dump raw generations on the user. It should help them decide.

### MVP Curation

- Group outputs by pack use case.
- Allow user favorites.
- Provide default labels:
  - Best LinkedIn
  - Best Website
  - Best Social
  - Best Press
- Manually tune label logic at first.

### Later Curation

- Face quality score
- sharpness score
- background quality score
- identity consistency score
- auto-ranking

## Phase 6: Exports and Finished Assets

Each pack should include exports that feel complete.

### Export Presets

- LinkedIn profile: square crop
- LinkedIn banner: wide crop
- X profile: square crop
- resume/avatar: small square crop
- website/team page: consistent portrait crop
- story/reel cover: vertical crop
- transparent cutout: PNG
- social post graphic: image + simple branded frame

### MVP Approach

- Generate/download original image.
- Add simple CSS/canvas crop presets in UI.
- Add server-side export generation later if needed.

## Phase 7: Concierge Workflow

Concierge is important for premium pricing and early learning.

### Concierge Promise

“Upload your photos and choose your pack. We personally review the outputs, regenerate weak results, and deliver the best finished images.”

### Manual MVP

- Add checkout option for concierge.
- Notify admin after purchase.
- Admin reviews user uploads and generated outputs.
- Admin marks final picks and sends delivery email.

### Benefits

- Higher AOV.
- Better customer outcomes.
- Fast learning about what users actually value.
- Differentiation from OpenAI direct.

## Key Product Principle

The user should feel like they are buying a finished outcome:

- “my LinkedIn photo”
- “my realtor profile set”
- “my founder press kit”
- “my team headshots”

Not:

- “credits”
- “prompts”
- “generations”
- “access to GPT Image 2.0”

## Decision Recommendation

Start with **three paid packs**:

1. LinkedIn / Executive Pack — broad demand, easy to explain.
2. Realtor Pack — strong niche, clear business ROI, good partnership potential.
3. Founder / Press Kit Pack — higher-ticket, strong for Alan's network and LinkedIn content.

Keep Job Seeker, Dating, and Team packs in the roadmap, but do not overbuild all six before validation.

## Definition of Done for MVP

- Three pack offers are visible on the site.
- Each pack has clear deliverables, price, CTA, and example outputs.
- User can select a pack and complete a purchase or concierge request.
- Upload flow explains how to get good results.
- Generation uses pack-specific recipes rather than generic prompts.
- Results are grouped/labeled by use case.
- At least one manual or automated delivery path exists for paid users.
- Analytics track CTA, checkout, upload, and purchase events.
