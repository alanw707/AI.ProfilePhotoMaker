# ADR 0011: Market briefs are deterministic, cited readings of pinned BLS releases

Status: accepted (2026-10-05, ticket #382, spec #376)

## Context

#382 saves a readable brief for the confirmed occupation (ADR 0010): occupational wages and employment nationally and near the goal location, projected outlook, role alternatives, source definitions and honest unavailable states. The owner chose free public data only. Later pay (#383–#384) and market comparison (#385) work reuses the same reference.

## Decision

- **Reference snapshot.** `AI.ProfilePhotoMaker.API/Data/Reference/bls-snapshot.json.gz`, built deterministically by `scripts/career/build-bls-snapshot.py` from two public-domain BLS releases:
  - OEWS May 2025 (`oesm25all.zip`, published 2026-05-15, USDL-26-0725): cross-industry, detailed-occupation rows for the nation, 51 states/DC and 393 metropolitan areas (177,513 rows): employment, employment PRSE, jobs per 1,000, location quotient, mean, 10th/25th/median/75th/90th annual percentiles, hourly median, annual-only/hourly-only flags.
  - Employment Projections 2025–35 Table 1.2 (published 2026-08-27): national employment 2025/2035, change, average annual openings, 2025 median wage, typical entry education/experience/training (831 occupations).
  - Values are a number or a BLS status, never a stand-in zero: `*` wage not available, `**` employment not available, `#` at or above the top code ($239,200 a year / $115.00 an hour); a missing row is "not published", and so are wages BLS does not publish for the occupation's pay basis (hourly wages of annual-only occupations such as teachers, annual wages of hourly-only occupations such as actors), so they are never reported as "too few responses". The snapshot carries each source's publisher, reference period, publication date, URL, definitions URL, SHA-256 of the source file, licence, citation, wage/employment definitions and coverage. The loader verifies the snapshot SHA-256, the public-domain licence and the presence of both sources, and fails closed per source.
- **Crosswalk.** O*NET-SOC code → published code: the exact SOC detailed code, else its SOC broad group (`xx-xxx0`, OEWS publishes some occupations only at that level); otherwise the occupation is "not published" by that source (11 of 893 for OEWS). When multiple O*NET occupations share a published detailed SOC code, the mapping is `shared`, not `exact`; a unique detailed code is `exact`, and the broad-group fallback is `broad`. Broad and shared mappings are disclosed in the brief by published code and title, including per-item notes for related occupations; exact mappings add no mapping note.
- **Location.** The goal's free-text location is resolved by `MarketAreaResolver`: `City, ST` matches a metro whose title lists that city and state (city names compared ignoring case and dots, "Saint" as "St", a trailing "City" or leading "Urban", and splitting titles on `-` and `/`, so "New York City", "Louisville", "Saint Louis", "Honolulu" and "Washington, D.C." resolve); a state code or name matches the state; `remote` or blank uses national only. Local figures use the metro, else the state; an unrecognised location makes local sections `unavailable` with reason `location_unresolved` and a next action to fix the goal location.
- **Brief as a run task.** `market_brief` (ADR 0009 runtime) needs a goal with a confirmed occupation (409 `CareerOccupationRequired`). It reads no profile text beyond versions, calls no model and spends no allowance. Steps: `read_goal`, `look_up_wages`, `look_up_outlook`, `compare_alternatives`, `save_brief`. Each section is built independently; a failing source marks only its sections `failed` (with a code) and the brief `partial`, keeping completed sections, with recovery action "start a new brief". Everything is saved in one fenced save with the run's completion.
- **Sections stay separate.** `wages` (benchmark wages: national and local, with the local–national median difference in dollars), `employment` (employment stock and concentration), `outlook` (projected national change and openings), `alternatives` (up to three other candidates from the confirmed match with their national median wage and projected change), `nextAction`. Copy states that these are occupational benchmarks: not open jobs, not a personal salary prediction, not total compensation.
- **Every figure is cited.** Each figure is `{ key, label, value, status, unit, areaCode, areaTitle, sourceId }`; the brief lists its sources with reference period, publication date, definitions and coverage. Figures are copied from the snapshot as published (no rounding beyond BLS's own) and fixture tests check every figure against the snapshot.
- **Staleness.** A brief pins the profile version, goal version, occupation and both source releases. When the goal or profile moves on (or the occupation changes), reads report `stale: true` with reasons; the old brief is kept unchanged. A source more than `Career:Market:DataStaleMonths` (default 18) past its publication date is flagged `dataStale`.
- **Analytics and report detail.** `/app/career/market` (alias `/app/career/analytics`) is the analytics view for this release: recent briefs plus the national/local comparison of the open brief (`?brief=`); each section shows its as-of date and coverage, every figure opens its source, local state figures are labelled as a fallback, and a failed run or partial brief offers "Try again". Multi-market comparison arrives with #385.
- **Privacy.** `CareerMarketBrief` carries `OwnerId`, cascades on account deletion, joins `CareerPrivateDataService` coverage and follows the run retention rule (kept while the account exists; export with #392); the page states this under the brief.

## Consequences

- Updating to a new BLS release means re-running the script, updating the expected SHA-256 and the fixtures; existing briefs keep the releases they were built from.
- The 5.4 MB snapshot ships in the API assembly; national/state/metro coverage only (no nonmetropolitan areas or territories in this slice).
